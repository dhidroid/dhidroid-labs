## The problem

Reviewing candidate profiles against a role description at any volume is slow and inconsistent. When AI tools assist with this, they often produce scores or rankings without explanation — which makes it impossible to verify whether the reasoning is sound or whether bias has been introduced.

The result is that reviewers either ignore the AI suggestion or accept it without scrutiny. Neither is good.

## What we explored

### Criteria-first review

The process begins with defining the role criteria before any candidate is evaluated. The reviewer specifies what matters — required experience, specific skills, indicators of fit — and the system uses those as the input, not a vague job description.

This separates the question "what are we looking for?" from the question "how well does this profile match?" Answering both at once invites motivated reasoning.

### Explanation alongside every suggestion

For each profile, the system shows the specific passages it used to form a judgement, alongside the judgement itself. If a candidate scores highly for "API experience," the reviewer can see which lines from the profile led to that assessment.

If the evidence is weak or missing, the score should be low. If the score is high but the evidence is thin, the reviewer can override it — and should.

### Human decision at every step

The system makes no final decisions. It surfaces and organises information. Every shortlist, every pass and every rejection is made by a person. The AI role is to reduce the volume of reading, not to replace the judgement.

## Boundaries

This is an independent prototype. It was not tested with real candidate data, and no conclusions were drawn about its performance on a real hiring process. The scoring approach is a demonstration of the explainability principle, not a validated model.
