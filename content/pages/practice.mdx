## Start close to the problem

Good work begins with a useful question. Before choosing a stack or drawing a screen, we take time to understand who the work is for, what is getting in their way, and what a better day would look like.

### 01 — Listen and frame

We gather the context, name the constraints, and make the unknowns visible. A clear first question gives the work direction without pretending we already know every answer.

### 02 — Make something real

We build the smallest version that can teach us something. A working sketch, a narrow prototype, or one complete slice is often more useful than a large plan built on assumptions.

### 03 — Learn from using it

We look at what happens when the idea meets real use. Feedback, edge cases, and awkward moments help us decide what to keep, change, or leave out.

## Keep the reasoning close

Decisions make more sense when their context travels with them. We write down the important trade-offs, keep code readable, and leave notes that help the next person understand why the work took its shape.

That care shows up in small things: a clear setup guide, an error that explains what to try, a focused test, and a scope that gives the important part room to work.

## Leave a useful next step

Every piece of work should make the next step easier to see. Sometimes that means a tool someone can keep using. Sometimes it means a tested assumption, a sharper question, or a decision the team can move forward with.

We value useful progress, honest learning, and foundations that are easy to build on.
