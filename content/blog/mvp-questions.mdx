An MVP is a test, not a smaller version of the final product. These seven questions help decide what belongs in it.

## 1. Who is the first user, and what are they trying to get done?

Name a specific person or role, not a market. "Small business owners" is too broad. "A florist who tracks orders in a notebook and loses jobs because she can't quote quickly" is a starting point.

## 2. What do they do today?

The existing workaround — even a spreadsheet or a WhatsApp group — shows what the product has to beat. If people are managing something complex in a spreadsheet, the product needs to be simpler *and* more powerful on the dimensions that matter. Don't underestimate the spreadsheet.

## 3. What is the one outcome the first release must deliver?

If you cannot say it in a sentence, the scope is too wide. "Users can submit and track a repair request without calling us" is a scope. "A complete customer management platform" is not.

## 4. What would you need to see to call it a success?

Choose a measurable signal before building. Not "people like it" — something you can observe. Task completion rate, return visits, support ticket volume, or time saved per week.

## 5. Which assumptions carry the most risk?

Every product idea rests on assumptions. Test the riskiest ones first. If the assumption is "users will pay for this," find out before you build anything. A landing page and a conversation cost less than three months of development.

## 6. What can be done by hand for now?

Manual steps behind the scenes are fine at this stage. If the product needs to send a weekly email, an actual person can send it at first. Build automation only once you know the shape of the thing.

## 7. What happens after launch?

Decide who fixes bugs, who handles support, and who decides what comes next. An MVP without an owner after launch is a liability, not a product.

---

If you can answer all seven clearly, you have enough to scope the first build. If some are still fuzzy, the discovery conversation is the right next step — not a sprint.
